What Is a Fermi Problem?

A puzzle you solve with smart guessing — not exact math.

Meet Enrico Fermi 👨‍🔬

Enrico Fermi was a famous physicist who could estimate almost anything in his head.

His most famous question: "How many piano tuners work in Chicago?"

Most people say "I have no idea!" But Fermi would calmly break it down:

🏙️ Chicago has about 3 million people.
👨‍👩‍👧 With ~3 people per household, that's about 1 million households.
🎹 Maybe 1 in 20 households owns a piano → 50,000 pianos.
🎵 Pianos get tuned about once a year → 50,000 tunings/year.
🔧 A tuner does ~4 tunings per day, ~250 days per year → 1,000 tunings/year per tuner.
🎯 So Chicago needs about 50,000 ÷ 1,000 = 50 piano tuners.

The real answer was around 150 — Fermi's estimate is in the right ballpark!

The Big Idea 💡

1. You don't need the exact answer — you just need to be close enough.

2. Break a HARD question into a few EASY questions.

3. Use stuff you already know (population, prices, rough sizes).

4. Multiply and divide your way to an answer.

Why Bother? 🚀

Scientists, engineers, and business people use estimation every single day:

🚀 NASA: "How much fuel will we need?"
💰 Startups: "How big is this market?"
🏭 Factories: "How many widgets per hour?"
🌍 Climate: "How much CO₂ in the atmosphere?"
🎮 Game design: "How long will players play?"

Estimathon Scoring 🎯

In estimation, getting the right order of magnitude is what counts. Here's how we score guesses:

🎯 Within 2× of the answer 10 pts
🌟 Within 5× of the answer 7 pts
👍 Within 10× (same order of magnitude) 5 pts
😅 Within 100× (off by 1-2 orders) 2 pts
❌ Way off (more than 100×) 0 pts

Example: Real answer is 1,000. Guessing 800 = 10 pts (within 2×). Guessing 200 = 5 pts (within 10×). Guessing 5 = 0 pts.
