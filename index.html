What Is Language?

A language is a set of sounds + rules that carry meaning between minds. The study of how it all works is called linguistics.

The 5 Levels of Language 🪜

Linguists break language into layers, from tiny to huge:

👄
Sounds (phonology) — the noises your mouth makes: p, a, sh…
🧩
Syllables & Word-pieces (morphology) — sounds clump into chunks: cat, un-, -s
🧱
Words (lexicon) — chunks become words with meaning: cat, run, happy
🔀
Sentences (syntax) — words line up in order: The cat runs.
💭
Meaning (semantics) — sentences point to ideas in the world.

Amazing Language Facts 🤯

🌍 There are about 7,000 languages alive today.
👶 Every healthy baby can learn any human language perfectly.
🔊 Humans can make ~150 different speech sounds, but each language uses only a handful.
🧩 With just ~40 sounds, English builds over a million words.
🆕 People invent languages too! Elvish, Klingon, and Dothraki are real "conlangs".
🐝 Even bees have a "language" — they dance to share directions!

Quick Check ✅

Sounds 👄

Every word is built from speech sounds. There are two big families: consonants and vowels.

Consonants vs Vowels

🛑 Consonants

You block or squeeze the air with your lips, teeth, or tongue.

p, t, k, m, s, l…

🌬️ Vowels

Air flows freely — you just change your mouth shape.

a, e, i, o, u

Why It Matters for Your Language

When you invent a language, you first choose its sound inventory — which consonants and vowels it's allowed to use.

Hawaiian uses only ~13 sounds (so words like aloha, mahalo). Some languages use 100+!

Words 🧱

Sounds clump into syllables, and syllables build words.

Syllable Patterns

We write C for consonant and V for vowel. A syllable is a pattern of them:

CVba, mi, to
CVCcat, dog, sun
CCVsto, gra, flu
CCVCstop, frog, spin
VCat, in, up

Some languages only allow simple CV syllables (Japanese, Hawaiian). English allows monsters like CCCVCCC ("strengths"!).

Word-Pieces (Morphemes) 🧩

The smallest meaningful pieces are called morphemes. We glue them together:

cat + s = cats (suffix: "more than one")
un + happy = unhappy (prefix: "not")
re + play + ed = replayed (again + past)

Sentences 🔀

Words must line up in a certain order to make sense. That order is the language's syntax.

Word Order Around the World

Most sentences have a Subject (who), a Verb (action), and an Object (what). Languages put them in different orders:

SOV~45%"I apple eat" — Japanese, Korean, Latin
SVO~42%"I eat apple" — English, Spanish, Chinese
VSO~9%"Eat I apple" — Irish, Hawaiian, Arabic
other~4%VOS, OVS, OSV — rare and exotic!
