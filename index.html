What Is Randomness?

Some things you can predict. Some things you can't. The unpredictable ones are random.

Predictable vs Random 🎯

📐 Predictable

  • If you drop a ball, it falls.
  • 2 + 2 always equals 4.
  • The sun rises tomorrow.
  • A clock ticks once per second.

🎲 Random

  • The next coin flip.
  • Which card you'll draw next.
  • Where lightning will strike.
  • Whether it'll rain at exactly 3:14 PM.

Expected Value (the Average) 📊

The expected value is the average if you played many times.

For one die: E[X] = (1+2+3+4+5+6) / 6 = 3.5

3.5 isn't a face on a die — but it's the long-run average!

Now let's add the values of two dice. The sum can be 2 through 12.

A triangle! Why? There's only one way to get 2 (1+1), but six ways to get 7 (1+6, 2+5, 3+4, ...). The middle is most likely!

Many Dice: Look Who Shows Up! 🔔

Now let's sum many dice at once. Watch the shape change as we add more dice…

As you add more dice, the shape becomes a bell curve 🔔! This magical fact is called the Central Limit Theorem.

The Bell Curve 🔔

Also called the Gaussian or Normal distribution. It's everywhere in nature!

Where the Bell Curve Shows Up 🌍

📏

📝

🌧️

Two Numbers Describe It 🔢

μ (mu) = mean — the center of the curve. Where the peak sits.

σ (sigma) = standard deviation — how spread out it is. Big σ = wide curve. Small σ = tall and narrow.

The 68-95-99.7 Rule 📐

For any normal distribution, this rule always holds:

±1σ ~68% of values fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean
±2σ ~95% within 2 standard deviations
±3σ ~99.7% within 3 standard deviations (almost everything!)

Example: heights have μ ≈ 5'7" and σ ≈ 3". So ~68% of people are 5'4" to 5'10", ~95% are 5'1" to 6'1".

Quick Check ✅

The Galton Board 🌰

A famous machine that turns coin flips into a bell curve, right before your eyes!

How It Works ⚙️

1. A ball drops from the top.

2. At each peg, it bounces left or right (50/50, like a coin flip).

3. After bouncing through many rows, it lands in a bin at the bottom.

4. Drop many balls, and the bins fill up like a bell curve!

Spot the Fake! 🎯

People are terrible at being random. Can you spot the real coin flips vs the fake ones?

The Trick 🧠

Real randomness has streaks — like 5 heads in a row. Humans usually avoid long streaks because it "feels" non-random.

So if a sequence has no long runs, it's probably fake!
